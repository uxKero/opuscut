# Pacing and transitions

## Pace is a choice, not a default

Videos made by code drift toward one pace: a new scene every bar, everything moving all the time, quick cuts to the end. That is one pace among many, and repeating it is exactly what makes these videos look alike. Choose the pace from the subject, the concept and what the user asked for, and write it in `video.md` before building.

| Pace | What it feels like | Built from |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Contemplative | One idea given room | Very few shots or one take, slow camera, long holds, soft transitions, silence and ambience |
| Measured | Calm and confident | Few scenes, each held until it is fully read, eased moves, cuts on phrases |
| Flowing | One continuous journey | A single world the camera travels through, morphs and match cuts instead of hard cuts |
| Lively | Warm and busy | Scenes on the musical grid, playful motion, a few quick accents |
| Frantic | Urgency, comedy, overload | Many cuts, bursts and impacts, holds used only as punctuation |

Mixing is allowed: a contemplative opening can break into a lively middle. The point is that the contrast is chosen.

## Time comes in beats

Count the video in beats and bars even with no music, so changes land where they are meant to. A slow pace uses long bars and changes rarely. Nothing has to happen on every beat. With narration, the voice sets the clock: size each moment to its phrase and change in the pauses.

## Shot lengths

- **Few shots is a valid edit.** One continuous take, or three long shots, can carry a whole video when the camera and the action inside the frame change.
- **Holds.** A long shot is where the viewer reads, looks and feels. Put the key message in a hold.
- **Contrast.** When you do cut often, vary the lengths. A row of equal shots reads as a slideshow. The ratio between the longest and the shortest shot, the spread in `analysis.md`, measures that variation.
- **Bursts.** Shots under about a quarter of a second read as impacts, not as shots. Use them only when the pace calls for impacts.

With a reference, take its pace from `analysis.md`: shot count, spread, cuts per 5 s and motion energy. A reference with four cuts in 30 s is a continuous film, and the video should be one too.

## Shape of the whole

- **Opening.** The first seconds say what this is. That can be a strong still image just as well as motion.
- **Peak.** If the video builds, one moment carries the most weight. A contemplative video may have no peak, only a turn.
- **Rest.** A pause in picture, sound or both makes the next moment land, at any pace.
- **Ending.** Settle into a hold long enough to read the last line.

## Directions and vectors

- **One current.** Choose one dominant direction of travel, such as left to right, into depth or downward, and keep most moves on it.
- **Reserved directions.** Save the other directions for meaning: up for a reveal, toward the camera for arrival, against the current for a problem.
- **Exit sets entry.** How a scene leaves decides how the next arrives. For a seamless move, accelerate out and decelerate in, so that the fastest points meet at the cut.

## Choosing a transition by meaning

| Transition | It means | Suits |
|:--|:--|:--|
| No transition, the camera keeps going | Same world, same moment | Flowing and contemplative films |
| Dissolve or slow crossfade | Time passing, memory, calm | Contemplative and measured films |
| Morph | This becomes that | Transformation, cause and effect |
| Match cut | This is that | A shape or object that continues into the next idea |
| Push through an element | Going inside | Zooming into a screen, a document, a detail |
| Hard cut | Now, a new fact | Most paces, and comic timing |
| Mask wipe | A new chapter | A change of topic on a downbeat |
| Iris or shape reveal | An opening or a closing | Openings, endings, focus on one subject |
| Flash or impact frame | A hit | Frantic and lively peaks only |
| Glitch | Something is wrong | Errors, hacks, instability |

Use one or two kinds of transition per film. Many kinds flatten each other.

## Attention over time

- **One focal point per beat.** When a word, a product and a symbol all need the eye, give them separate beats or a deliberate handoff, not one crowded frame.
- **Every element has a job.** Each line, ring, panel or label carries information, shows a real relationship, or becomes part of the next transition. Remove the rest.
- **Impact is not speed.** A slow reveal can hit hard. Decide the impact and the pace separately.

## Type that performs

When a word owns the frame, give it an action that means something, not a routine entrance:

- **Takeover.** The word grows past the frame and becomes the next scene's field. Hold it readable before the crop.
- **Mask edge.** The word rises through a precise edge, and that edge becomes the next shot's boundary.
- **Handoff.** One word or letter stays in place while the sentence changes around it.
- **Choreography.** Letters fall, stretch or reassemble, then settle into readable spacing.

Use one or two of these per film. Repeating them turns them back into a template.

## Images and products move with intent

Every featured image, screen or product gets its own motion: how it enters, what changes that earns attention, and where it settles. A still held for its whole shot, or the same gentle zoom on every asset, reads as a slideshow. Check that the asset can do what the motion promises. A flat image can slide, mask or split into depth layers, but it cannot turn to show a new side. For that you need a 3D model or footage, so say which one you are using.

## Motion within a shot

- **A verb for every element that moves.** Choose it from how things behave: settle, drift, float, breathe, unfold, draw, fill, grow, slide, drop, stamp, snap, slam. Pick the verb from the concept, not from an energy level. Gentle verbs are as valid as violent ones.
- **Stillness.** Not everything moves. Motion reads against stillness, and a frame where only one thing moves is often the strongest.
- **Ease to the material.** Heavy and calm things ease slowly and do not overshoot. Rubber and toys can bounce.
- **Stagger.** When several things enter, vary their timing and direction so they do not arrive as one block.
- **Consequences.** Plan what an action changes, not only what appears.
